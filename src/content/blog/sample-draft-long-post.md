Long posts benefit from a little navigation. On this post, the table of contents appears beside the article on larger screens and collapses neatly on mobile.

The headings below exist only to demonstrate the layout. Replace this content with your own article.

## Plan the article before you write it

Start by writing the promise of the article in one sentence. Then list the questions a reader would need answered. Each question can become a heading.

This keeps the article focused and gives you a table of contents that is useful rather than decorative.

## Write for one reader

It is easier to write clearly when you imagine a specific reader. What do they already know? What are they trying to do? What would confuse them?

Write the first draft for that person. Edit the second draft for everyone else.

## Use headings as signposts

A good heading tells the reader what the section will help them do. Prefer concrete headings over clever ones.

- "How to choose a metric" is clearer than "The metric maze".
- "What to do after the interview" is clearer than "The waiting game".

Clear headings also make the table of contents more useful.

## Keep paragraphs short

Short paragraphs are easier to scan on a phone. If a paragraph runs to more than four or five sentences, look for a natural place to split it.

This is a style choice, not a rule. Some ideas need more room. The goal is rhythm, not uniformity.

## Add examples and caveats

Examples make abstract advice concrete. Caveats stop examples from becoming false promises.

When you use an example, say whether it is real, hypothetical or simplified. When you make a recommendation, note where it might not apply.

## Link to sources carefully

If you cite a source, link to it directly and name it. Avoid citing a number you cannot trace. If you are unsure, describe the idea without the number.

Sources are listed at the end of this sample so you can see the layout.

### A note on nested headings

The table of contents can include third-level headings. On very long articles, you may prefer to show only the main sections to keep the list short.

## Review before publishing

Before you publish, check:

1. Does the introduction state the promise clearly?
2. Does every section support that promise?
3. Are the examples honest and the caveats clear?
4. Do the internal links work?
5. Is the description useful in search results and social previews?
6. Is the publish date correct?
