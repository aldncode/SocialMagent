Not having paid client work does not mean you have nothing to show. It means you need to create evidence of how you think, how you work and how you communicate. A portfolio is a record of that evidence.

The goal is not to look experienced. It is to make it easy for someone to imagine you doing the job.

## Why a portfolio is not a highlight reel

A highlight reel says, "look at this result." A useful portfolio says, "here is the problem, here is what I chose to do, and here is what I learned." The second version helps an employer or client judge your judgment.

This distinction matters because early-career portfolios often try to compensate with busy visuals. A calm, specific case study is more persuasive than a folder of unrelated graphics.

## Pick one niche and one audience

Choose a type of business or a type of problem you want to work on. For example:

- social content for a local service business;
- lifecycle email for an online course;
- reporting for a small ecommerce brand;
- recruitment content for a growing team.

You do not have to commit for life. A narrower brief makes the work deeper and easier to explain. It also gives you a reason to research the audience properly.

## Show the thinking, not just the output

For each piece, include:

- **The brief:** what was the business trying to achieve?
- **The audience:** who needed to hear the message?
- **The constraints:** time, budget, approvals, available tools.
- **The choices:** why this format, channel, message and call to action?
- **The work:** the actual post, email, landing page or dashboard.
- **The learning:** what you would test next if you had more time.

If you use a fictional brand, say so clearly. There is nothing wrong with self-directed work. There is something wrong with implying it was paid client work when it was not.

## Build three small pieces of evidence

Three focused pieces are usually enough to start a conversation. They can be:

1. **A content plan** for a fictional or real small business, with a sample week of posts and a short rationale.
2. **A simple analytics review** using a public dataset or a self-created spreadsheet, showing how you would turn numbers into decisions.
3. **A campaign concept** with one core message, a channel plan and a clear measurement approach.

Keep each piece short enough to read in ten minutes. A portfolio that takes an hour to understand is unlikely to be read closely.

## Write a short case study

Use a simple structure:

> **The problem.** A short paragraph about the situation.
>
> **What I did.** The decisions you made and why.
>
> **What I made.** A visual or link to the work.
>
> **What I would measure.** The metrics that would show whether it worked.
>
> **What I learned.** One honest reflection, including what you would change.

The "what I would measure" section is especially useful for candidates because it shows that you understand measurement before you have a real result to report.

## Where to publish it

A clean one-page portfolio site is enough. You can also publish case studies as posts on LinkedIn or as PDFs. The important thing is that each link opens quickly, works on a phone and does not require a login.

If you use a template, edit it until it sounds like you. Recruiters can tell when every paragraph has been written by the same service.

## Keep it honest

Do not invent client names, awards, revenue figures or results. Do not use a real company's logo in a speculative project. Do not describe a group project as your solo work.

Honesty is not a weakness in a portfolio. It gives you a stable story to tell in an interview, and it protects your reputation when someone checks.

## A final check

Before you send your portfolio, ask:

- Can someone understand what I actually did within two minutes?
- Is it clear which work was paid, which was self-directed and which was collaborative?
- Does the work connect to the roles I am applying for?
- Are the links working and the files accessible?
- Does the writing sound like a person?

You do not need a perfect portfolio. You need a clear one.
